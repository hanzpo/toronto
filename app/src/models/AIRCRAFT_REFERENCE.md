# Aircraft reference (models/aircraft.ts)

Dimensions, drawings and livery notes behind the airliner models. Compiled 2026-09-30.
Units: metres / degrees; `x` is measured aft from the nose tip. Tags: **S** printed in a
manufacturer airport-planning document, **S\*** sourced number whose meaning was inferred
from an unlabelled drawing, **D** derived, **M** measured off the manufacturer drawings
(≈ ±0.3 m / ±3°), **E** estimate. Every section lists its source URLs.

Primary sources: Airbus *Aircraft Characteristics – Airport and Maintenance Planning*
(A220 ACP, A320, A330, A350), Boeing *Airplane Characteristics for Airport Planning*
(737NG/MAX, 757, 767, 777, 787), Embraer / MHI RJ / De Havilland airport planning manuals
(E175, E195-E2, CRJ900, Q400), ATR 72-600 factsheet, Wikipedia spec tables. Reference photos
(Wikimedia Commons side views) and 3-view crops were used to measure nose profiles and
cockpit windows; they are not committed (copyright) — the file names are listed per section.

## How the specs map to the model

- `AircraftSpec` fields (aircraft.ts) use the sourced numbers directly: length (fuselage
  length `fus.len` where the ACAP overall length is measured to the stabiliser tip), span,
  height (fin tip), fuselage W × H, belly clearance, wing root LE x / root & tip chord /
  LE sweep / dihedral / TE kink, tip device, engine intake-lip x, lateral position, nacelle
  diameter / length / centre height (from ground clearance), fin & tailplane geometry, nose
  gear x, wheelbase, track, wheels per main leg, retraction direction, doors (centre x),
  window pitch.
- **Nose profiles** (`fus.prof`, crown / keel / half-width at 10 stations) and **cockpit
  panes** (`panes`, metres): Q400, ATR 72-600, A320 family and 737 were measured by hand
  from the reference photos (crops of the Porter Q400, Qantas A320, Hapag-Lloyd 737-800
  side views). CRJ900, E-Jets, A220, 757/767, 787, A330, A350 and 777 profiles were
  **generated by OpenAI Codex (gpt-6-astra)** from the same photos + these dimensions
  (`aircraftNoses.ts`), then reviewed in the model gallery against the photos.

## Distinguishing features (what each model must show)

| pair | how to tell them apart (modelled) |
|---|---|
| **Q400 vs ATR 72-600** | Q400: 32.8 m long slender tube (fineness ≈ 12), long wedge nose with a low sharp tip and flat chin, windscreen band set back high under a smooth roofline; **T-tail** with bullet fairing; deep nacelles hung under the high wing running **well aft of the trailing edge** and housing the main gear (retracts **aft** per the Q400 landing-gear course notes and Wikipedia — the lead's brief said forward; the sourced direction is used); 6-blade Ø4.11 m props far ahead of the wing; twin small nose wheels ≈1.8 m aft of the tip. ATR: 27.2 m stubby Ø2.87 m tube, short blunt snub nose with a low forward windscreen under a brow, **low tailplane at the fin root**, short nacelles ending at the wing TE, **main gear in fuselage-side sponsons**, 6-blade Ø3.93 m props. |
| CRJ900 vs E175 | CRJ: long pointed nose, small high windows, **rear-mounted engines + T-tail**, low stance, short gear. E175: rounded double-bubble nose, 4-pane cockpit, **under-wing engines**, conventional tail, large canted winglets. |
| A320 vs 737-800 | A320: blunt round nose (tip near the centre line), 6-pane cockpit with the V-shaped lower sill and notched rear pane, sharklets, round CFM/IAE nacelles, main gear bay doors, cone APU tail. 737: pointed nose on the centre line with a long drooping chin, 3 panes per side with a flat sill, blended winglets (MAX: split tips), **flattened-bottom CFM56 nacelles** close under the wing (MAX: round, chevrons), **no main-gear doors** (wheels exposed in the belly), long dorsal fin. |
| 787 vs A330 | 787: smooth sloping nose with **4 large panes**, raked wingtips (no winglet), chevron nacelle nozzles, large windows. A330: Airbus 6-pane cockpit, canted winglets (-900: curved sharklets), long-duct nacelles. |
| A220 | Smooth slender nose, 4-pane cockpit, very large geared-fan nacelles for the fuselage size, small canted winglets, exposed main wheels. |
| 777-300ER | Huge Ø4.2 m nacelles, **6-wheel main bogies**, raked tips, blade tail cone. |
| A350 | Drooping A380-style nose, curved wingtips, black cockpit mask (livery), 4-wheel bogies. |

## Model detail / animation (summary)

Landing gear with bay doors retracts per flight phase (nose forward, mains inward — Q400
aft into the nacelles, ATR into sponsons, 737 / A220 / E175 / CRJ without main-well doors),
flaps + slats deploy with speed on approach / takeoff, ground spoilers on the landing roll,
props spin. See docs/AIR.md § Rendering.

**Windows**
- Window size is 28 × 41 cm (11 × 16 in), larger than the A320's (WP).
- The -300 has **17 windows ahead of the overwing exit** (-100 has 13) (ALS).
- Total is ≈38 per side and pitch is ≈0.55 m (E, counted and scaled from the photo refs/BCS3_side.jpg).

### Cockpit windows
The A220 has a **4-piece windshield** (ALS): two large front panes plus one large side window each side. There is no eyebrow row and no small notched third pane, unlike the Airbus A320 6-pane layout. The panes sit low in a long, smoothly tapered, pointed nose (ACP p.113 drawing; photo).

### Recognition cues
- The PW1500G nacelles look very large relative to the slim 3.5 m fuselage. They are mounted well forward, with the intake lip ahead of the wing root.
- The nose is long, pointed and drooped, with the 4-pane windshield.
- The winglets are short and canted about 45°, not a tall sharklet.
- The main wheels are exposed in the belly (no main gear doors).
- The fin is tall and swept with a large dorsal fillet. The horizontal tail is low on the upswept cone, which ends in a blunt APU exhaust.
- Five-abreast windows are large, square-ish and closely spaced.
- There are two cabin doors per side plus one overwing exit per side. The -300 is told apart from the -100 by 17 windows (vs 13) ahead of the overwing exit.

Neo nacelles are about 0.3–0.5 m larger in diameter than CFM56 nacelles. They hang further forward and higher, very close to the wing leading edge, and have 0.46 m ground clearance compared with 0.58 m.

### Fuselage shape (E, scaled from AC320 p.48)
- **Nose:** Blunt, rounded Airbus nose. The fuselage reaches constant section about 6 m aft of the nose tip, around door 1. The cockpit windows sit high on the nose taper, with the windshield base about 1.4 m below the crown.
- **Tail cone:** The keel starts sweeping up just aft of the aft cargo door, about 11–12 m ahead of the tail tip, at roughly 12–13°. The crown starts tapering at the fin root. The cone ends in a vertical-knife-edge APU exhaust at about 4.7 m AGL (AC320 p.57 "AP").
- **Belly fairing:** Large belly fairing under the wing box, bottom at 1.62 m AGL (AC "BF1").

### Recognizability cues
- **A319:** Short fuselage and stubby look, with a relatively big fin for its length. Only about 11 m of fuselage separates the nose gear from the mains. Overwing exits: 1 per side on most aircraft (the AC drawing shows 2).
- **A320:** The baseline shape. 2 overwing exits per side, 2 main door pairs, wing roughly mid-fuselage.
- **A321ceo:** Long fuselage, with plugs both ahead of and behind the wing. **4 door pairs** (D2 just ahead of the wing, D3 just aft of it) and **no overwing exits**. The wheelbase is long, and the nose gear looks far forward.
- **A321neo:** The ACF version, the common one on recent deliveries, replaces D2 with **2 overwing exits per side**. D3 aft of the wing is optional and often deactivated or plugged. The non-ACF A321neo keeps the ceo door layout.
- **ceo vs neo:** ceo aircraft have small triangular **wing-tip fences** (late ceos may have sharklets). neo aircraft always have 2.4 m **sharklets** and **much larger, rounder nacelles** that nearly touch the wing leading edge. LEAP and PW nacelles both look fat. The CFM56 on ceo aircraft has a flat-bottomed intake, and the V2500 has a long thin nacelle.
- **All types:** Pointed-cone APU tail, low-set horizontal tail on the tail cone, twin-wheel mains without a bogie, and a nose gear that retracts forward.

### Cockpit windows
Six-pane windscreen (two large near-rectangular front panes plus two side panes per side, the aft-most with a swept/angled rear edge), derived from the A380 nose [S Wiki]. Almost all operators paint a black band around the window set that wraps back and up behind the side windows — the "raccoon mask" / "sunglasses" look — the single most recognisable A350 cue. Nose is long, smooth and pointed (A380-like), with no visible eyebrow windows.

### Recognition features
- Black "raccoon mask" around the cockpit windows; long, drooping A380-style nose.
- Smoothly curved, upswept blended wingtips (not a separate sharklet fence) — continuous curve up over the final ~4.4 m; very high-aspect wing with visible in-flight flex.
- Very large Trent XWB nacelles (≈3.7 m) hung well forward of the LE; no chevrons.
- Four-wheel main bogies retracting sideways into the fuselage with fully closed bay doors; long belly fairing.
- Wide round fuselage (5.96 m), 4 pax door pairs, door 3 immediately aft of the wing.
- Strongly upswept, tapered tail cone with APU exhaust; tall fin (≈9.4 m) with modest TE sweep.

### Modelling notes
- The ACAP "overall length" is measured to the **stabiliser tip** where that is the rearmost point (777, 787, and the 737 planforms). The fuselage/tail-cone length is the separate side-view dimension.
- Ground-clearance tables give min/max over loading states; the nominal is about the midpoint. The 737 tables list MAX (OEW) and MIN (MTW).
- The door x values are **door centrelines** (the NG/757 tables state "nose to center of door").

## Airline livery reference (simplified, shader-paintable)

Conventions: "fin" = vertical stabiliser, heights as fraction of fin height from root (0) to tip (1), chord positions 0 = leading edge, 1 = trailing edge.
"Belly split" = height of the belly/upper boundary as fraction of fuselage height (0 = keel, 0.5 = wing-root / mid-fuselage, ~0.62 = window line).
Hex values: brand-guide values where cited, otherwise `~` = eyeballed from photos (good enough for a shader).
Confidence: [H] verified from photo/official source this session, [M] from memory + partial source, [L] memory only, verify before relying.
Titles are NOT drawn (per spec) — the Porter, Sunwing and FedEx fins are essentially lettering, so fallbacks are given.
